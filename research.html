Iron Cycling along Continental Margins

I investigate how sedimentary inputs, particle dynamics, redox chemistry, organic ligands, and ocean circulation regulate the transport of iron from continental margins to the open ocean. Using high-resolution physical–biogeochemical models, my research examines how iron released from low-oxygen sediments is transformed, retained, or transported offshore by upwelling, boundary currents, and eddies.

Our simulations show that approximately 20% of sediment-released iron is transported offshore, highlighting the importance of fine-scale ocean circulation and biogeochemical processes in supplying iron to the Northeast Pacific.

Related publication: Pham et al. (2024). The shelf-to-basin transport of iron from the northern U.S. West Coast to the Pacific Ocean . Global Biogeochemical Cycles, 38, e2023GB008029.
