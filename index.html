Anh Le-Duy Pham, Ph.D.

Welcome

I am a chemical oceanographer and marine biogeochemist specializing in ocean trace-metal cycling, aquatic chemical processes, and biogeochemical modeling.

My research combines observations, numerical models, and data synthesis to investigate how redox reactions, organic ligands, sedimentary inputs, particle dynamics, and ocean circulation regulate the distribution and biological availability of iron and other nutrients.

I develop and apply regional and global ocean biogeochemical models to connect chemical processes with marine ecosystems, carbon cycling, and climate change.

I received my Ph.D. in Oceanography from the School of Earth & Atmospheric Sciences at Georgia Institute of Technology in 2019 and my B.S. in Marine Engineering from Hanoi University of Science in 2013.

I am currently a Postdoctoral Scholar in the Department of Atmospheric and Oceanic Sciences at the University of California, Los Angeles (UCLA). Before joining UCLA, I was a Postdoctoral Associate at the Laboratoire d'Océanographie et du Climat (LOCEAN) at Sorbonne University in Paris.

Research

Iron Biogeochemistry

I investigate the processes controlling the marine iron cycle, including redox chemistry, organic ligand binding, scavenging, sedimentary iron inputs, particle dynamics, and biological uptake.

California Current System

My research at UCLA focuses on the California Current System and the eastern Pacific Ocean. I combine observations and high-resolution numerical modeling to investigate the transport and cycling of iron along continental margins and its connections to ocean ecosystems.

Iron and Marine Ecosystems

I study interactions between iron and marine organisms, including heterotrophic bacteria and zooplankton, and investigate how iron availability and recycling influence marine food webs and ecosystem dynamics.

Ocean Models and Observations

I use regional and global ocean biogeochemical models together with observational datasets and model intercomparison to understand processes controlling ocean biogeochemistry.

Marine Carbon Cycling

I am interested in the interactions between trace-metal cycling, marine carbon cycling, benthic-pelagic coupling, and climate-change feedbacks.

Research Projects

Iron Cycling along Continental Margins

My research investigates how sedimentary inputs, particle dynamics, redox chemistry, organic ligands, and ocean circulation regulate the transport of iron from continental margins to the open ocean.

California Current System

I combine regional ocean modeling and observations to investigate physical and biogeochemical processes controlling iron distributions along the U.S. West Coast and their implications for marine ecosystems.

Iron and Marine Food Webs

I am investigating how iron availability and recycling influence heterotrophic bacteria, zooplankton, phytoplankton, and higher trophic levels.

Global Iron Model Evaluation

I evaluate ocean biogeochemical models using observations and large-scale data synthesis, including comparisons between model simulations and GEOTRACES observations.
